## 2. The M2 testset was never executed, and did not pass as written

Three defects, all in the test, fixed in fork commits `93ef848` and `1efdc6f`; no property and no tolerance changed:

1. The wire blocks are nested per channel and per plane; `wire_rows` now flattens each channel before comparing.
2. Two comparisons broadcast `abs` over a `Vector{ComplexF64}` (`maximum(abs.(pair_forces))`), which errors.
3. The hand-built request forced `quadrature_order = 1, singular_order = 1`, coarser than the app's payload. On the
   4-triangle tetrahedron that destroys reciprocity. Measured through the real Python payload on the same fixture:
   **3.7e-4** reciprocity error at 100 Hz with the coarse orders and **2.2e-7** with the app's defaults; in-test the
   coarse orders gave **31.6 %**. Removing the two overrides (the app never sets them) is the fix; a finer fixture
   was not needed (n = 1, 2, 3 subdivisions all agree to ~1e-7 with the defaults).


Pass: `channel_pressure` has the C4 shape, the force matrix (Z row-scaled by the full-domain tag area) is symmetric to
<= 3.2e-4, and the legacy fields are channel 0.

**An earlier run of this check failed at 22-38 %, and the cause was mine, not the solver's:** I drove the quarter mesh
with the fork directly and never set the symmetry flag, so a quarter shell was solved as a closed body. The app
adapter derives the flag from the mesh (`check_symmetry_pairing`), and with it the same check passes. Recorded here
because a 22-38 % reciprocity error would otherwise look like a solver defect.

The area-mean pressure matrix itself is NOT symmetric (it differs by the area ratio 4.77, i.e. 79 %), which is the
expected reading of C4: mean pressure = force / area, and only the force is reciprocal.

`notch 0.000`, `rip 0.694`.

What the screen says, stated narrowly:

- **T2 beats T1 on J** (9.53 vs 13.23 at screen, 11.89 vs 14.51 at verify), consistent with the dossier's claim that
  the 2 x 6.5 in complement is the higher-output one. This is a comparison of two different briefs' J values, so it
  ranks the two typologies' own searches, not two designs in one objective.
- **Both winners push the driver toward the mouth and the ports low** (`slide_mm` 35-36 of the -10..40 range,
  `z_frac` 0.09 for T2 and 0.26 for T1). The optimiser is using the top of the slide knob; that is worth a study
  before any of it is called a design law.
- **`lw_resid` dominates both J values** (7.8 of 9.5, 13.1 of 13.2): the listening-window response is far from the
  target. `notch` is exactly 0 in both (no narrow dips), so the penalty is broad-band, not a resonance.
- **`n_nan 12` is not a metric failure.** `metrics.json` for the T2 winner has finite on-axis values at all 12
  frequencies (`n_nan_ripple 0`); the 12 NaNs are the synthesis's deliberately-NaN impedance array.

**Two gaps this exposes, both real:**

1. **The screen did not run over the brief's band.** The solved grid was 12 frequencies from 500 to 10,000 Hz
   (`run --dry-run` line, and the run's `freqs_hz`), while the brief asks for 120-12,000 Hz. The objective therefore
   scores `n_in_band 10` of those points and never sees 120-500 Hz or 10-12 kHz. The contracted 1/6-octave
   120-12k grid has NOT been run.
2. **The verify mesh equals the screen mesh** (the CLI says so in its own line); only the frequency count and the
   balloon size change. So "verify" here is a re-score at more frequencies, not a finer mesh.

**Link 3 detail.** Two runs. (a) A CD-only BEM sweep (tag 2 alone, 8 frequencies 500-4000 Hz) rises to 37.1 dB at
1250 Hz and then falls; no notch, because at L1 an undriven entry port is a rigid patch. (b) The physically correct

response (4.82 s, xo 673.7 Hz, order 2), the mid channel muted to -300 dB, then `system_response`. On axis the summed
CD + passive-port response is:

```
f=  900.0 SPL0=  26.281    f= 1000.0 SPL0=  27.075    f= 1100.0 SPL0=  26.181
f= 1200.0 SPL0=  24.929    f= 1300.0 SPL0=  34.115
```

The notch is 9.19 dB deep below the 1300 Hz point, and the 1200 -> 1300 step is 9.19 dB, so the centre lies between
1200 and 1300 Hz: +18.7 % to +28.6 % of the 1-D 1010.8 Hz prediction. The +-20 % gate is met only at the very bottom
of that interval; the likely centre (~1250 Hz, +23.7 %) is outside. Placing it needs a 25 Hz sweep across
1150-1350 Hz. Verdict: PARTIAL - the passive-port notch exists, is deep, and is in the predicted region, but it is not
yet shown to be inside +-20 %.

**Link 4 detail.** The inner optimiser's per-evaluation cost meets the budget (5.4 ms against the 2.5 ms measured in

optimiser. The 2 s gate in the hand-off is therefore missed by 3.3x; the fix is a budget/combo decision (fewer
evaluations, pruned driver combinations, or a per-combination cap), not a solver change. It is small next to the
43-52 s BEM solve per candidate.
