## Throat and upper-frequency verification

The repo's prior 2-inch tests used a 50.8 mm idealized exit and 2° model assumption. FaitalPRO lists HF1440 as a 35.8 mm combined exit without a published numerical angle. The HF206 and HF2000 are 50.8 mm drivers with a published 30° conical exit and 900 Hz minimum crossover. The 15° half-angle case is an explicit interpretation for a geometry sensitivity experiment. Verify the included-angle convention on a drawing or sample and obtain a real source characterization before labeling the simulation as a specific driver. FaitalPRO HF146 is a useful 35.6 mm, 29° conical alternative to investigate if upper-band width wins on the smaller exit. The BMS 4592ND and B&C DCX462 coaxial 2-inch drivers have separate MF/HF radiators and need a different source treatment and their own crossover. Do not infer polar response from marketing frequency range.

- The default v11 search ends at 8 kHz. The new [12 kHz](../../../examples/briefs/home_xo900_2026-11-20/room_landscape_80x50_900_hf1440_v11cd_verify12k.yaml) and [16 kHz](../../../examples/briefs/home_xo900_2026-11-20/room_landscape_80x50_900_hf1440_v11cd_verify16k.yaml) verify-only briefs extend coverage and CD bands for the 1.4-inch finalists. CPU verify meshing passed V4 and Morph at both bands. At 16 kHz they used 12,924 and 15,672 DOF, so confirm CUDA budget, mesh convergence and real driver/source behavior at finalist scale before claiming the upper band. These are distinct diagnostic score identities.
- For the 2-inch finalists, first establish whether the exit-angle treatment improves the actual 8 kHz polars at the same 900 Hz loading goal. A separate extended-band verify brief and a driver-aware model or physical polar measurement are needed above that. The ideal-source results only measure the simulated horn and boundary combination.
- Pucker is a controlled inward radial offset from a reference wall. The roll at a free-standing mouth and moving a lip fore or aft change different coordinates. Inspect the inner H meridian and its flare rate, the actual 3D termination, and matched polars. A front rendering cannot show whether a particular curve meets the dispersion target.
