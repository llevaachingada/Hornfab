# HornFab: data-backed off-axis ripple, resonance and HF coverage review

**19 November 2026 | For Fable senior agent | Read-only audit, no solver reruns or repository edits**

## Executive diagnosis

The November 19 archive contains **actual solver responses**, not merely PNGs. I accessed and decoded **16 distinct `response.npz` archives** in `docs/evidence/runs/scout40_2026-11-19/`, examining their original H/V SPL cuts, complex throat impedances, and in selected cases their paired scoring records and geometry knobs. Each inspected response had 18 frequencies and 73 points per H/V cut; the response files additionally store 512 balloon directions and complex pressure. The scans show a specific discrepancy: some designs have low scalar J and low same-frequency angular-ripple scores while their fixed-angle **off-axis frequency responses** have pronounced structure. This warrants a new diagnostic/validation layer; it does **not** yet prove an acoustical resonance or require rewriting all objective weights.

### Quantitative evidence

An **exploratory spectral-irregularity metric** was computed from each actual `spl_v_db` array. At a fixed +25° or +35° vertical angle, subtract the 0° SPL at each frequency, fit a quadratic trend against `log2(f)` over 1,065–4,832 Hz (10 actual solver frequency samples), and report RMS residual in dB. A quadratic removes broad smooth narrowing; it is not a validated hearing-weighted score, nor is it directly equivalent to the HornFab `rip` objective. These examples are not a pooled J leaderboard because the B3/C200 variants use `v7_smooth` and the OSSE/R-OSSE group mostly uses regular v7.

### Specific defect that J is missing

Klangfilm B3 current, V +25° off-axis relative to 0°:

- 1,763 Hz: **−6.9 dB**
- 2,269 Hz: **−6.7 dB**
- 2,573 Hz: **−3.6 dB**
- 2,919 Hz: **−3.6 dB**
- 3,311 Hz: **−4.9 dB**

Simultaneous complex normalized throat impedance `z_throat`:

- 2,269 Hz: **0.966 − 0.122j**
- 2,573 Hz: **1.016 − 0.135j**
- 2,919 Hz: **0.983 − 0.136j**

The pattern change has **no corresponding large impedance feature at these sampled frequencies**. It may be spatial redistribution, multiple-mode interference, or diffraction, but an unobserved narrow resonance is also possible. It is unwarranted to diagnose mouth resonance solely from the heatmap.

The archive's original scoring code (`hornlab_search/metrics/smoothness.py` and objective `scalar_v7.py`) primarily penalizes detrended **angular irregularity at a fixed frequency**. It does not directly penalize **frequency-domain deviations at a fixed listening angle**. The newer v10 repairs a v9 ripple-region error; that repair alone does not add fixed-angle spectral consistency. The archived SCOUT40 campaigns searched v7, not v10.

### Best HF geometry is not yet the best 700 Hz horn

**Crowe X OSSE** is a valuable *acoustic direction*, not a confirmed winner. It provides approximately 74° H / 54° V at 8 kHz and smooth off-axis cuts. Its recorded geometry uses a rolled `osse_rollback` kernel, early wall angles H ≈ 36.39° and V ≈ 22.71°, length ≈ 260 mm, and lip radius ≈ 18.3 mm. However its estimated `lfp50_hz` ≈ 986 Hz and `rez_min_from_fx` ≈ 0.342 are weak for the intended ~700 Hz crossover. Its normalized `load` score term ≈ 2.638 accounts for a large share of its J ≈ 5.345 under the current regular v7 loading weight of one.

**OSSE morph** shows a stronger loading/HF balance (8 kHz ≈ 60° H / 50° V; `lfp50` ≈ 504 Hz), while **extended throat** has ≈ 62° H / 56° V and `lfp50` ≈ 412 Hz. The extended-throat design has a different effective source/throat-geometry setup, so confirm its driver coupling and avoid attributing cross-family differences to one shape knob.

**Research hypothesis, not proven:** preserve Crowe X OSSE's relatively broad and spectrally consistent upper-range radiation while modifying expansion/load-bearing geometry to recover 700 Hz loading. Test geometry changes under controlled, source-identical conditions rather than assuming a particular lip, wall angle, or morph setting is causal. Conical/early high-angle development and transitions are plausible, but the present comparisons are across whole typologies and do not isolate a knob.

## Proposed Fable review mandate (senior discretion)

1. **Reproduce and check the data audit.** Use the bundled `hornfab_spectral_ripple_audit.py` on the committed archive (or adapt it). Validate cut and complex-pressure identities, check symmetric ± angles, compare `response.npz` to `metrics.json`, verify stable rankings under small changes to spectral detrending and angular set, and preserve objective/brief identities. No new BEM is required for this step.
2. **Build a rigorous measurement taxonomy.** Separate angular ripple, fixed-angle off-axis spectral variation, DI/beamwidth instability, detached lobes, modal interference, throat/mouth reflection, and real-driver/wall resonances. Design meaningful diagnostics before writing new weighted terms. Compare against Sridhar/Tylka/Choueiri's five constant-directivity metrics and own horn-specific targets; don't require artificially constant directivity from a legitimate smoothly narrowing horn.
3. **Validate anomalies and frequencies.** B3's vertical feature near 2.3–3.0 kHz is a good first test. Re-solve a short *densely sampled frequency interval* with a finer mesh; check convergence, not just interpolation. Compare complex pressure, phase, throat impedance, and source/sampling settings. A frequency-domain BEM with 18 sparse points cannot establish time-domain ringing; for CSD/impulse response, use an appropriately dense uniform frequency grid and trustworthy phase/propagation conventions.
4. **Use a controlled HF-width experiment.** Preselect matched variants of Crowe X OSSE, R-OSSE rectangular and OSSE morph. Test early flare angle/timing and source-to-horn transition independently from mouth rollback and aspect/morph. Record 4, 6.2, 8 and (only once adequately meshed and source-qualified) 10–16 kHz coverage alongside LF loading, fixed-angle spectral residual and severe lobe/notch diagnostics. Search toward a Pareto frontier, not a single arbitrary J.
5. **Check installed source validity.** Above the ~5.6 kHz first transverse-mode threshold for the nominal 35.8 mm throat, the uniform-velocity source is a particularly important assumption. Verify representative winners against a credible real-driver wavefront or measurements before treating an 8–16 kHz improvement as physical truth.
6. **Then revise scoring if justified.** The first product should be reproducible *report-only* metrics and a disagreement table. Rank reversals versus J, repeatability across frequency/mesh fidelity, and relationship to acoustic failure determine which metrics should become objective terms or qualification gates. Do not retroactively redefine historical J or mix briefs.

This does **not** dictate a particular geometry family, modal decomposition, filter design, tolerance, or ranking formula. Fable should select methods and modify the hypothesis if the validation contradicts the exploratory findings.

## Reproducibility notes and boundaries

- `hornfab_spectral_ripple_audit.py` is a standalone **read-only** local script to run in a HornFab checkout. It is not already installed in HornFab, does not rescore historical J, and does not need Julia/BEM/GPU.
- All illustrative spectral RMS numbers fit ten **actual** samples from 1.065–4.832 kHz. The polynomial detrend may understate broad deviations, while sparse frequency samples may **entirely miss** narrow resonances. Different detrending/angle assumptions must be sensitivity-tested.
- The BEM model is rigid-wall, linear, and uses a simplified source. It does not establish printed-wall vibration, phase-plug cavity behavior, thermal losses or subjective audibility.
- The source and geometry data make the Crowe X OSSE HF result a useful measured simulation result, **not proof** that its wall angles/lip radius independently caused the improvement.
