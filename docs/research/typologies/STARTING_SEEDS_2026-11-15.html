1. Executive summary

What we are deciding

Which horn family gets the next GPU search night, and under which scoring brief. The owner wants a Crowe ES-450-style home horn: landscape, X/bow-tie creases, thick rolled lip, about 1.8:1, depth about 0.45 x width. The room/box allows 600 W x 480 H x 480 D mm, and the crossover is 700 Hz with a Faital HF1440 1.4-inch driver.

Current status: new solves are paused. The night-E runner and the running Crowe-section job were stopped at ~62/96 rows. The GPU was cleaned. We are not queueing night F until the brief and the geometry law are settled.

The three blockers

  1. Scoring brief. The v7 objective has a fixed flat-DI target and a fixed-round diagonal reference. The listening literature does not support a flat-DI target as a universal goal; the “Harman 1–3 dB/octave” claim did not survive a source check. We need a deadband or a defensible target, and a decision on diag.
  2. Geometry law. The current laws have no true under-surface lip return and no waist term. The search can make a clean Bi-Radial, a clean rounded rectangle, or an X-on-a-CD-throat, but not Crowe’s thick bead/waist. The owner’s instinct that the curves/parameters are not right is correct.
  3. Construction defect. The Crowe-section family has a 153–176° fold with inverted triangles at the outer side-wall/lip junction. The inner flare is smooth, but the night-E scores are provisional.
What we need from you: look at the six 3D seeds and the sections; say whether the priority is the Crowe look or the best chance of a good-sounding 700 Hz horn; approve the brief direction (deadband vs target and how to treat diag); and approve the lip/waist geometry work (and the Crowe-section junction rebuild) before any new solves.

ES exponential spiral

3. How to read the sections

Horizontal section (cut through the middle)

This is the wall profile looking down from above, through the horn’s centre plane. The horizontal axis is the distance across the horn; the vertical axis is depth from the driver face. The two close lines are the inner and outer wall (3 mm wall thickness); where they separate at the mouth, you are seeing the lip/termination.

What to look for: does the wall leave the throat smoothly, or is there a kink? Does it flare continuously, or turn into a cone? At the mouth, does it end at a sharp edge, a rounded edge, or return under itself? The return is the “lip” question.

Vertical section (cut through the middle)

This is the same cut in the vertical plane. It shows the mouth height, the vertical flare, and the top/bottom lip. A large vertical mouth is what buys low-frequency vertical pattern control; the 480 mm box height is the hard limit.

What to look for: the vertical mouth height and depth, the smoothness of the top/bottom wall, and whether the lip returns. The Bi-Radial and R-OSSE rectangle end at a clean edge; the Crowe-X/ES end in a thin flange or hook; the Crowe sections have a side-wall junction fold.

What the sections do not show: sound. They show the shape that the BEM solver will see. A smooth section is necessary but not sufficient: the mouth size, the coverage angle, the throat loading, and the diagonal pattern all matter. The issues log below separates geometry facts from acoustic evidence.

How to think about a search range: a narrow range around a good seed is an exploitation run; a wide range is an exploration run. The search starts at the seed with a step of about 20% of each range and tries 96 candidates. If a range includes geometry the law cannot build, the candidate is refused or penalized; if a knob only changes the drawing, the search may still move it because the score has shape proxies. The box checks above are there to keep the refusal fraction low; the brief is there to tell the search what “good” means. Both matter as much as the optimizer.

6. What the outside research says

Full annotated bibliography and confidence labels: HORN_WHITEPAPERS_2026-11-15.md. The short version:

Coverage and mouth size. Keele’s CD horn uses an exponential throat, a conical midsection whose included angle sets the HF beamwidth, and a bell that fixes midrange narrowing. The Bi-Radial adds independent H/V wall pairs and the mouth rule. The corrected rule is W = 25,000/(0.9 B F); at 700 Hz, 80° H = 496 mm, 50° V = 794 mm. The 600 mm H mouth is enough; the 480 mm V mouth is not, so the vertical will be wider than target at the crossover. [US4071112; US4308932A; JBL 2380A data]

Mouth termination. Geddes’ verified rule: a radius of at least a quarter wavelength removes most edge diffraction — 35 mm is clean above ~2.46 kHz, 50 mm above ~1.72 kHz, 86 mm above ~1 kHz. A 40–50 mm lip is a rational hi-fi choice. The 270° wrap is a Crowe claim; no independent measured/BEM comparison of wrap angles was found. A 90–130° roll already terminates the profile. [Geddes, Loudspeaker System Design Philosophy; ATH TwinRay roundover study]

Waist and X creases. The waisted/bow-tie outline has no acoustic source; ATH’s morph target is a convex rounded rectangle. Corner creases are curvature discontinuities (HOM/diffraction risk per Geddes) and partly a morph consequence (ATH says the morph adds a shifted diagonal). Treat them as visual until diagonal polars exist. [Geddes; ATH Tritonia]

Narrowing. Le Cléac’h FEA shows DI rising from ~4 dB at 1.4 kHz to ~12 dB at 12 kHz (~2.6 dB/octave), smooth off-axis and clean decay. Crowe’s own ES-600 report shows stronger narrowing (45° down 4–10 dB above 2 kHz) and a 1 kHz resonance needing a passive network. A smooth 2–3 dB/octave rise is acceptable if monotonic, continuous at the 700 Hz crossover, with a coverage floor and no in-band resonance. [Sheerin; Fourcade; Crowe]

Scoring. No primary/credible secondary source was found for a “Harman preferred 1–3 dB/octave” DI slope. Olive’s 2004 model uses NBD_ON, NBD_PIR, LFX and SM_PIR; no DI-slope term. CTA-2034’s 12/44/44 is the PIR synthesis average, not a preference weighting. The v7 flat-DI target and fixed-round diag reference are not supported for a smoothly narrowing or rectangular/creased horn. [Olive 2004; Toole/Olive; CTA-2034; source check]

Analysis. 6 EPW is a screening mesh (~10% frequency error); verification should use 8–12 with a convergence check. Use balloon DI and full polars, not cuts-only; extract Re Z and absolute level; couple a lumped driver for loading. On-axis-only optimization can create deep off-axis minima. [Kolbrek; Boundary Lab solver report; COMSOL; AKABAK]

Optimization. CMA-ES is a solid baseline; TuRBO/GP-BO is competitive and more sample-efficient. Keep 2–3 objectives, treat refusals as constraints, verify the top finalists on the dense grid. [Hansen; TuRBO; Smolen & Halley 2021]

Measurement. A printed waveguide can show 1–4 kHz artifacts from secondary transmission paths; a sand layer fixes the measurement. Baffle-edge diffraction and mic-boom reflections corrupt wide-angle data. The driver exit limits HF directivity (~8–9 kHz on a 1.4-inch driver). [JASA 2025; MTG waveguide shootout]

Do not cite the “Harman 1–3 dB/octave” claim as fact. It did not survive this source check. Treat a smooth 2–3 dB/octave rise as acceptable under the conditions above, not as a published target.

Glossary

CD: constant directivity; the horn tries to hold the same coverage at all frequencies.

DI: directivity index; how much louder the on-axis direction is than the average of all directions. A rising DI means the beam is narrowing.

Flare: how the wall opens from throat to mouth.

Loading: the acoustic impedance the horn presents to the driver; more loading = the driver works against a stiffer air column at low frequencies.

Lip / termination: what happens at the mouth edge; a rounded or rolled lip reduces edge diffraction and mouth reflection.

Throat: the small end at the driver; the blend from the driver exit to the horn contour must be smooth and round.

Waist: a concave/bow-tie mouth outline; a visual consequence of Crowe’s panel construction, not an acoustic requirement.

X creases: diagonal ridges from the throat to the mouth corners; a Crowe visual signature and a potential diagonal-polar risk.
