## Crowe construction and model identity

**ES-600, published/observed:** sheet 2 labels 417 mm width, 203 mm height, 198 mm depth and 35.560 mm throat. The front marks B-B horizontal and C-C vertical. The H section B-B marks 17° between two inclined construction lines and C-C marks 12°. They are included angles in the drawing, but whether they specify exact local throat tangents or another section construction is not established just from the rendering; do not map either directly to a single universal round-driver angle. Different markings make an assumed common H/V profile particularly unsafe. The wood hatched C-C shows substantial top/bottom material with a rounded front surface; B-B and oblique show a curved, extended body and junction rather than only a thin lip. The front shows lens-shaped top/bottom regions and curved meeting lines; this image does not prove the complete 3D join is C1/C2 or explain its construction. Rear indicates a mounting plate/holes, not source-wavefront details. The drawing is sheet 2 of 2; no interpolation between views can certify off-axis return, normals, clearances or the internal source connection. The article calls the wrap 270° and attributes diffraction/resonance improvements to it; this is an author claim without a wrap-angle control in this audit. It calls the width/height golden ratio, but 417/203=2.054, not 1.618. Other dimensions, hidden constructs, or naming cannot resolve that numerical discrepancy without further evidence.

**ES-800, observed/published:** drawing 1054 shows 297 × 120 × 146 mm for the original horn; its listing gives 1-inch throat. Its apparent 328 mm dimension relates to the installed/cabinet arrangement, 141 mm to the installed assembly, and 340/600 mm to cabinet geometry. Section A-A shows the wide plane with a curved exterior behind the profile; B-B shows thick separate upper/lower meridional returns and the attached driver. Do not transfer those dimensions to ES-600. The current No.3073 product is 300 × 152 × 140 mm; the separate ES-800 article describes approximately 320 × 120 mm. These are variant/description discrepancies, not rounding evidence that the original CAD dimensions changed. The product explicitly says detailed curve dimensions are unavailable. ES labels alone do not establish a common exact parameter law.

**ES-250, published:** No.2213 eight layers of 45 mm has full horizontal return but a partial vertical wrap ending at vertical, 800 × 360 × 549 mm, 50.8 mm throat. The ten-layer version adds 90 mm to height and full top/bottom wrap; 450 mm height follows by arithmetic, not an independent full-model drawing dimension. Separate configurations should be recorded separately. The manufacturer calls the vertical partial choice a height/driver-spacing tradeoff, not an acoustically proven superior return. The ES-600 section angles and scales should not be grafted onto it.

The primary examples show nontrivial rear structure and direction-dependent return; a meridian that folds back, a thick bead, a closed and valid exterior, and an integrated panel junction are four distinct properties. Neither a smooth front view nor H/V agreement proves watertightness or a valid off-plane junction. Crowe calls the proprietary flare exponential spiral, but the sources examined do not give its parametric equation, cross-section interpolation or exact tangent field. A fitted logarithmic spiral, a rolled rectangle, a section-loft and a solid panel-body model are hypotheses, not reconstructed exact Crowe CAD.

## ATH definitions and discrepancies

In OS-SE pp. 2–7, phi is azimuth measured from x, z is axial distance, r0 is **radius**, alpha is nominal **half** beamwidth and alpha0 **half** the included throat angle. Generalized OS profile plus superellipse termination is `r(z) = sqrt((k*r0)^2 + 2*k*r0*z*tan(alpha0) + z^2*tan(alpha)^2) + r0*(1-k) + (s*L/q)*(1-(1-(q*z/L)^n)^(1/n))`. Angles in equations require a consistent trigonometric unit conversion; ATH config fields are degrees while azimuth expression p is angular. `s` controls termination amount, `n` its progression, `q` truncates the superellipse (typical paper range .99–1), whereas R-OSSE rev7 **q is a throat-shape mixing exponent**, not the same parameter. R-OSSE x(t), y(t) are separate axial/radial functions, t in [0,1]; it can return in x, which single-valued OS-SE r(z) cannot. Rev7 describes R as outer radius, a nominal half-coverage, r0 throat radius, and a0 half-throat opening; example a0=7.5° gives 15° included. Separate imposed wall thickness/closure is needed to specify an entire solid; the paper's inner profile is not Crowe's body.

The paper §5 radial morph is `rm = r + ((z-zf)/(L-zf))^gamma * (rM(phi)-r(L,phi))` for z≥zf, unchanged below. At gamma=1 the onset can have a slope discontinuity; gamma>1 makes it more gradual. `Morph.AllowShrinkage=0` means ATH may enlarge the requested target, so expected result is not always the requested numeric target; the manual's example uses 1 when forcing shrinkage. User guide rollback angle is a desired **terminating wall angle relative to horn axis**, with rollback start based on **distance along the curve**, not axial distance. It is not automatically the curve parameter extent or Crowe's verbal wrap angle.

**Tritonia original:** source page gives `alpha(p)=48.5-7*cos(2p)^5-16*sin(p)^12` and `s(p)=0.7+0.2*cos(p)^2`, `k=1,n=3.7,q=0.995`; then rounded-rectangle morph. At p=0°,45°,90° the nominal alpha values are 41.5°, 48.25°, 39.5° and s=.9,.8,.7. These are pre-morph profile parameters, not measured 83°,96.5°,79° final beamwidths. Original config specifies L=135 mm, **r0=36.0 mm**, a0=4.2°, target rectangle, fixed fraction 0, rate 3, corner radius 18 mm, ABEC.SimType=1, H/V and 42° inclination diagonal, 2 m distance and 10° report normalization. OS-SE defines r0 as radius, implying 72 mm diameter, while the author calls the original a 1.4-inch throat (~35.56 mm diameter). The raw script is ambiguous/inconsistent with the page and must not be quietly 'fixed' or used as HF1440 proof; obtain author clarification or an independently verifiable original model. Tritonia-F is full scale but revised to 1-inch driver, so it cannot resolve the exact old script. Page describes original IB optimization; its enclosure advice and simulated polars have that context.

The repo original hybrid described in `ROLLED_FAMILY_MASTER_RESEARCH_2026-11-15.md` uses a windowed inward radial offset of existing rolled or section bases, while `tools/geometry/section_pucker.py` is signed axial displacement. These are different from each other, from Tritonia's angle-dependent OS-SE profile, and from its subsequent radial morph. A zero-control recovery and source-preserving windows are useful identity tests, not evidence of matched published geometry. Neither the accidental twisted ancestor nor an unspecified angular displacement is the published final Tritonia.

## Validation cases and unresolved evidence

1. Freeze source identities. For ES-600 compare dimensioned extents, 35.56 mm aperture and B-B/C-C silhouette/relative positions at the original drawing scale, with image digitization uncertainty and full body vs flow aperture distinguished. Preserve 17°/12° as separate drawing annotations pending exact line interpretation. Compare intermediate cuts, derivatives, thickness, underside and junction against actual authorized 3D CAD if obtained; drawings alone cannot supply exact truth or justify sub-millimetre tolerance.
2. For ES-800 check 297 × 120 × 146 mm and 1-inch throat against drawing 1054/old listing, and compare the two section shapes and complete body, not the installation dimensions or No.3073. For ES-250 retain partial/full identity and 360/derived 450 mm heights and horizontal versus vertical return as qualitative tests. These are geometry checks, not acoustic validation.
3. OS-SE reference cases: set s=0 and k=1 to recover the stated generalized OS equation; at z=0 recover r0 and, for regular n>1, initial slope tan(alpha0); p above checks angular law. Compare direct ATH output and an independently evaluated implementation using identical angle units, profile settings, morph target, shrinkage policy and mounting; the 4.7 guide §5 rounded-rectangle example reports raw target 254 × 216 mm and forced 400 × 200 mm with shrinkage on. Check before/at/after zf, endpoint and derivative behavior. Published config throat discrepancy means a full Tritonia attribution check is blocked until resolved.
4. Rev7 pp. 3–4 supplies independent R-OSSE equations; verify x(0)=0, y(0)=r0, y(1)=R under valid parameters, and sample the returned curve at multiple t values against an independently evaluated reference. Note the paper's typical parameter set and actual settings before comparison; compare same tip termination and mounting. Derivative, intersections, winding, minimum wall thickness and closure require full 3D checks; profile return alone is insufficient.
5. Source tangency and radius need verification at every azimuth and correct driver reference. Matching tangent does not guarantee equal curvature or wavefront: rev7 pp. 21–22 compares a conical driver duct and predicts a reflection despite slope matching. Distinguish source-cap idealization from a coupled real driver. H/V/diagonal polar comparison must state baffle, model, driver, normalization and measurement distance; no exact polar or desired DI follows from the geometry alone. Do not initiate new solves under the existing hold.

**Remaining limits:** CAD access, exact curve definitions and detailed whole-surface correspondence remain unresolved; front/oblique/underside source imagery does not supply numerical full 3D evidence. Author acoustic claims are not independent replicated results. The present audit made no measurement, mesh, acoustic solve or physical recommendation.
