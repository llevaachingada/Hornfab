## 1. Central geometric hypothesis and why earlier approaches miss it

From the owner's four photographs and the additional manufacturer and developer sources below:

- Driver mounting plane appears circular. The opposing folds visible looking down the horn may physically extend inside the **projected circular driver-exit envelope**, creating a narrowed waist. This is not the same as a ridge relative to an already expanded wall.
- An apparently scalloped opening can also arise from a circular tube joining the flare at different axial depths around its circumference. Perspective and nonplanar seams can be misleading. A centered axial projection smaller than the exit circle, with verified wall occlusion, is stronger evidence of actual intrusion. Photos alone do not establish the exact proprietary sections.
- In a true directional constriction, one pair of walls can move inward as the orthogonal pair expands. Total cross-sectional area can remain constant or increase. It is not necessary to squeeze the entire passage.
- The folds then recover and blend into the main flare, with diagonal surfaces independently shaped to avoid cross-shaped radiation. AudioHorn's MX modular throat suggests the complicated near-throat geometry can be separated from a simpler body; that is a useful engineering architecture, not proof that the commercial X and MX cores are identical.

Separate these operators and name them consistently:

| Term | Physical definition |
|---|---|
| Circumferential slot depth | z-position of local flare onset; can vary with azimuth without narrowing below the source radius |
| True directional constriction / pinch | acoustic wall at some forward z enters the projected source disk; `r(z,phi)<r0` for a round reference |
| Directional contraction | negative local radial derivative `dr/dz`, whether or not below r0 |
| Ridge / valley | signed local wall displacement relative to a stated parent surface, not necessarily contraction |
| Scalloped seam | nonplanar closed intersection of tube and flare |
| Diagonal meridian shaping | independent control away from H/V planes |
| Mouth beak / axial pucker | downstream lip displacement, not a throat feature |
| Source cap | excitation surface; never use as an acoustic wall when extracting throat sections |

The earlier Hornfab `18 -> 14.2 mm` apparent pinch was identified as a domed source-cap artifact in the repository, so do not repeat that mistake. The new feature must occur in the actual inner wall, after the source interface.

## 6. Proposed implementation: build independent mechanisms, not one giant family

Use physical azimuth `phi=0` horizontal, `pi/2` vertical, and z along the forward acoustic axis. Confirm current convention and convert once at the boundary. Work on the analytic/design inner surface first; reconstruct the outer wall consistently, then emit mesh. For a star-shaped forward core, represent `S(z,phi)=(r cos(phi), r sin(phi), z)`. Use true planar axial sections for area and throat visibility; do not confuse arc fraction with z/L. Keep rollback/mouth operator downstream of the throat blend and untouched in the first A/B comparison.

### 6.1 Operator A: angular slot depth / scalloped flare onset

Use a nonnegative, smooth function, e.g.:

```
D(phi) = D0 + DH*cos(phi)^(2*pH) + DV*sin(phi)^(2*pV)
             + DD*sin(2*phi)^(2*pD)
```

Use nonnegative components for first experiments; allow a signed Fourier form with explicit nonnegative D bounds later. For noninteger p use `abs(cos(phi))**(2*p)` etc., and guarantee regularity at zeros. H/V/diagonal coefficients interact; report actual depths on each plane, not just gene names. The two published ATH slot recipes can also be expressed directly or as analytic reference variants. This is *not* a negative radial operator.

An optional C2 delayed coordinate:

```
x = z - D(phi)
G_b(x) = 0                         if x <= 0
       = b*(t**3 - 0.5*t**4)       if 0 < x < b, t=x/b
       = x - b/2                   if x >= b
```

G is C2 at both ends. Its effective onset is offset by b/2 in the linear region. Do not use this blindly on the driver tangent: if the parent has a nonzero entry angle, retain the fixed matched entrance segment and introduce delayed *excess flare* downstream. Build a C2 join to the parent at the end of the core. Document how the mapping changes local slopes, preserves overall depth and mouth, and how ATH's actual slot differs from the smoothed variant. For genuine scalloped seam construct `r_t(z)=r0+z*tan(theta_t)` and solve `r_flare(z,phi)=r_t(z)` for the physical transition branch `zc(phi)`. If multiple/no roots, label or use a controlled smooth loft rather than arbitrary Boolean selection. Trim/join analytically or use CAD surface blending; a raw Boolean mesh often creates slivers. A max/union of flare and tube air volumes cannot produce a below-tube pinch; use Operator B/C independently.

### 6.2 Operator B: signed angular initial wall angle (BLD research reference)

```
a0(phi) = a0_base - PH*abs(cos(phi))**(2*pH)
                  - PV*abs(sin(phi))**(2*pV)
                  - PD*abs(sin(2*phi))**(2*pD)
```

This is the simplest direct experimental reproduction route for ST260BLD. In a profile formula, a negative a0 can create a negative initial `dr/dz`; check radicands and geometry throughout, not just endpoints. First match exact source recipe, then offer a distinct versioned *driver-matched* variant. For the latter, the source-plane driver tangent stays fixed and the inward turn begins after a short matching collar. Do not globally relax `a0_deg` or alter source-cap shape to fake a signed wall angle. If existing native formula only accepts axisymmetric angle or pin, use an explicit GRID forward core; log the distinction. Test exact CAD parity of BLD independently of source physics; do not assert a 25.4 mm BLD geometry with -27° source-wall tangent matches any particular compression driver.

### 6.3 Operator C: driver-matched localized radial sculpting (preferred production research)

A simple compact-support deformation independent of slot depth:

```
r_raw(z,phi) = r_base(z,phi) + r0 * w(z) * F(phi)
F(phi) = c2*cos(2*phi) + c4*cos(4*phi) + c6*cos(6*phi) + c8*cos(8*phi)
```

Use only c2,c4 initially. Negative c2 narrows H relative to reference, expands V. c4 alters cardinal vs diagonal shaping; signed values matter. Peak amplitude is relative to r0 and not necessarily actual pinch below r0; calculate actual min waist. For H and V mirror symmetry use only even cosine terms. For arbitrary rotation use full/half mesh as dictated by reflection symmetry and source; do not assume quarter symmetry for arbitrary phases. Alternative basis: `F= -PH*|cos(phi)|^(2pH) - PV*|sin(phi)|^(2pV) - PD*|sin(2phi)|^(2pD) + orthogonal recovery terms`. Independent angular width/shape allows narrow opposing lobes. Nonconvex section requires more than an ellipse; test curvature and hull deficit.

Use smooth compact-support w. Example with independent onset z0, peak zp, end z1:

```
Q(t)=6*t**5-15*t**4+10*t**3
w(z)=0                                  outside [z0,z1]
     Q((z-z0)/(zp-z0))                   z0 <= z <= zp
     1-Q((z-zp)/(z1-zp))                 zp < z <= z1
```

This is C2 with zero derivatives at start, peak, end. At the driver exit require base geometry and tangent identical; place z0 beyond source match or set z0=0 with zero first/second derivative. Keep the unchanged downstream join at z1. If the parent expands too fast for useful contraction, choose a smaller forward core / adjust base near-throat expansion in a separate controlled lane, rather than increasing c2 without regard for pressure or shell thickness. A local actual waist below r0 is the central test; a merely relative ridge is a different valid mechanism.

For area-preserving experiments normalize at fixed z:

```
A_raw(z)=0.5*integral_0^2pi(r_raw(z,phi)^2 dphi)
r(z,phi)=r_raw(z,phi)*sqrt(A_target(z)/A_raw(z))
```

Set `A_target` to the same parent area curve, or a separately specified monotone area law. Check positive radii before normalization; area normalization is not a repair for self-intersection. Re-measure actual pinch after normalization; control actual waist via root-find on amplitude where monotonic and feasible. Verify tangent and join derivatives after normalization. Compare free-area vs fixed-area and actual total-contraction arms, clearly labeled. For example, an equal-area ellipse narrowed to 70% of the exit width needs height 1/0.7=1.429 times exit diameter; with area 110%, height 1.571 times diameter. This is an area illustration, not the nonconvex target shape.

### 6.4 Operator D: section loft / independent guiding meridians (if A–C cannot capture reference)

Use a low-order smooth angular basis and an axial B-spline/Bezier representation, with independent H, V and diagonal controls. Fit actual extracted BLD contours first and use them to initialize an interpretable reduced basis. Consider area and normalized shape separate: `r=sqrt(A/pi) * q(z,phi)/sqrt(mean(q^2))`, with positive q; ensure source circle and slope exactly. Coefficients versus z can be fitted to measurements with smoothness regularization and fixed derivatives at endpoints. Alternatively use a spline contour for non-star-shaped sections, with consistent parameter correspondence and a valid Jacobian. If freeform lofting, compare cubic Bézier meridians with fixed endpoint/tangent controls as in Bezzola; one cubic may not express both a strong inward excursion and smooth recovery under all endpoint constraints, so piecewise splines may be better.

Do not call a 2-plane interpolation a full three-dimensional solution; independently model diagonal and intermediate profiles. Make it possible for the optimizer to learn that a visually strong ridge hurts performance. Add axial seam controls only when the simpler construction demonstrably cannot fit the reference.

- Absent/neutral block reproduces parent geometry, source and cache identity (if required by parent contract).

## 8. Meshing, source and numerical fidelity

At c=343 m/s, nominal wavelength and element edge-size guidelines:

| Hz | lambda mm | lambda/6 mm | lambda/10 mm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4000 | 85.75 | 14.29 | 8.58 |
| 8000 | 42.88 | 7.15 | 4.29 |
| 12000 | 28.58 | 4.76 | 2.86 |
| 16000 | 21.44 | 3.57 | 2.14 |
| 20000 | 17.15 | 2.86 | 1.72 |

These are starting guidelines, not convergence guarantees. Resolve both physics and geometry. Use local target `h = min(lambda/N, sqrt(8*epsilon/abs(kappa_max)), h_feature)` for chord error epsilon, max principal curvature and feature width/spacing, with handling of zero curvature. At least several actual elements across narrow ridges and blend widths; trial 0.5–1.5 mm near smallest throat features, 1–2 mm sculpted core, acoustic criterion elsewhere. Do not assume exterior may be arbitrarily coarse; finite baffle/free-standing radiation, lip diffraction and rear surface can affect response. Verify DOF cap does not silently defeat requested EPW or local curvature refinement. Candidate `favRBs__ridge8` had 24k DOF cap and n_phi25/n_s40; this may be too coarse for this new mechanism. Inspect actual exported wall triangles and sample grid independently. Check shape fidelity against analytic sheet and between refinements; outer offset normals can self-intersect around tight concave features. Use curvature-aware shell thickness or explicit manufacturing variant, not silent clipping.

Perform convergence at low/mid/high bands and around narrow resonances. Compare at least 6/8/10 EPW where computationally feasible, plus independently refine throat feature sampling. Report absolute/normalized response, diagonal lobes, impedance, J, mesh size, memory and runtime. Set tolerances before comparing, e.g. provisional target <0.5 dB typical on-axis difference, <1 dB relevant angular response, <2–3° beamwidth difference, with explicit outliers and impedance convergence; adapt to empirical repeatability. Never declare a gain smaller than mesh/solver noise. If current solver is not credible to 16 kHz, report verified band and blockers rather than treating unverified response as truth.

Source: ideal uniform normal velocity is useful for isolation; real phase plugs may have nonuniform complex velocity and nonplane output. Compare a realistic or parameterized perturbation source where supported; quarter symmetry may exclude odd modes and asymmetric source cases, so validate full model/symmetry reconstruction for selected finalists. Preserve exact source normalization (fixed volume velocity, pressure or input power as appropriate) and report chosen convention; compare absolute efficiency at equal excitation. At high power linear BEM does not prove low distortion, especially with small waist and local pressure concentration. For a circular hard duct, cutoff `f = c*chi'_mn/(2*pi*r)`: for 35.56 mm, m=1 first ~5.65 kHz, m=2 first ~9.38 kHz, next axisymmetric ~11.76 kHz. Symmetry and excitation determine whether those modes actually couple; a flaring noncircular section has different modes. Evaluate complex throat impedance and local pressure/phase, modal decomposition or FEM if possible; do not infer true loading from normalized SPL or group delay alone.

## 10. Scoring and success criteria: no principal-plane trickery

Do not replace current V11 scoring wholesale. First audit existing full-sphere/CD metrics, caches, angle-grid convention, objective bands, missing-data penalties and source normalization. Add throat-specific observables and independent oblique contour diagnostics with explicit metric versions. If current score ignores diagonals, compare diagnostics and create a separate versioned objective before optimizing against it. Use existing Princeton/generalized CD work in repo, not a new unvalidated scalar J.

For a tangent-plane elliptical target (one choice, not a mandatory physical law):

```
beta(phi) = atan(1 / sqrt(cos(phi)^2/tan(beta_H)^2
                         +sin(phi)^2/tan(beta_V)^2))
```

Here beta are half-angles measured from forward axis. For 80x50 full coverage, 45-degree diagonal full angle ~60 degrees (calculate exact from formula). This is not a universal optimum; an audience footprint/rectangular coverage target may prefer a different contour. Choose target from brief, evaluate multiple thresholds and disconnected contours, track worst oblique as well as mean error. Check actual 3D balloon, both spectral smoothness and total radiated power. Never let excellent normalized H polars conceal severe on-axis loss, bad oblique nulls, or impedance resonances.

Provisional gates (agent may revise and document):

1. **Reference geometry:** credible reproduction of ST260BLD signed pinch and angular slot references; quantify fit residuals, genuine wall occlusion, section widths, area curves; if downloads unavailable explicitly label reconstruction.
2. **Implementation:** neutral replay, source compatibility, geometry/mesh validity, tests passing, end-to-end active parameter authority. At least one genuine below-radius pinch in emitted inner mesh, not source cap or perspective.
3. **Numerical:** converged results for matched baseline and representative new cases including 12–16 kHz where supported. Document solver/mesh limits.
4. **Acoustic:** improvement on a stated practical target beyond convergence/seed variance; H broadening without unacceptable V widening, diagonal collapse, loading loss or new deep nulls. Track Pareto tradeoffs if no single winner. A negative result is useful; do not claim ridge benefit from its visual prominence.
5. **Packaging:** report actual envelope and printability, part joints, wall thickness, source mount. Prefer interchangeable throat module and fixed flare for physical A/B. Maintain separate acoustic and manufacturing feasibility.
6. **Physical:** prototype a small set of robust candidates and parent/control. Same driver, fixture, measurement distance/window and EQ; measure H,V, diagonal, additional obliques, impedance and multi-level distortion. No high-SPL claims from linear simulation alone.
