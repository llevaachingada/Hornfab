These rankings change substantially at the top even though overall ranks remain correlated; the v7-to-v9 difference also includes the different ripple window and other scoring changes.

Under all nine specified range/onset cases, combined strict-constancy rankings retain **15–20 of the base top 20**, with rank correlation **0.985–1.000**. The first-ranked stored observation changes under some shifts; no individual first place is robust to all choices. The constant-directivity baseline strict and slope-limited variants share **18/20** and **89/100** top observations; the answer depends on the provisional slope bound. No total is exactly zero with the three requested dead bands, or when additionally applying the DI-slope range; this leaves constancy and flat CV active and does not prove a fully dead-banded objective avoids a flat plateau. No design is recommended as a winner.

## Method and assumptions

The v9 canonical scorer is called directly on a `MetricSet` from stored response arrays. The canonical `objective/replay.py` currently does not dispatch v9. A material DI mismatch was found: the generic metric regeneration assumes equal-area balloon samples, whereas the original runs used weighted DI. `.s1/correct_di.py` replaces the two DI-dependent contributions with the corresponding terms from stored DI arrays on the identical grid. `.s1/verify_di.py` independently calls full v9 using stored DI for ten spread-out examples per cohort and checks the algebraic adjustment. Three sampled H and V regenerated beamwidth curves matched their stored arrays exactly; this is not a complete beamwidth parity audit. Response rows missing required cuts, impedance or balloon are excluded and counted. Original v7 J is retained as stored, separately comparing constant-directivity and smooth-brief cohorts because their stored objectives differ.

The placeholder corridor is H 60–80° from 700 Hz and V 40–60° from 1,000 Hz; H and V profiles extend to 8 kHz. V9 uses its own frequency weighting and the common home target. Its corridor-mode ripple window is the *difference between corridor endpoints*, here 20°, as implemented by the canonical v9 scorer. This can differ substantially from the v7 ripple window, so ranking changes do not isolate constancy alone.

Chosen scratch weights are v9 defaults aligned to the home brief: coverage 1, ripple 1, flatness 0.5, loading 1, DI slope 1, DI roughness 1, flat CV 1. Tolerances are 3, 1 dB, 1.5 dB, 0.3, 9 dB/decade, 0.5 dB, 1.5 respectively. H/V coverage weight is 1/0.5; narrow-side ratio is 2. These values are provisional, not an authorized scoring change. The added strict cost is weighted 1, with mean squared angular error divided by **25 deg²**, so 5° RMS costs one unit, matching 1 dB ripple under the selected weight/tolerance; V is half-weighted. This adds constancy to the existing per-frequency corridor and can charge an out-of-range curve in both terms.

The slope-limited variant uses a line against log-frequency with slope clipped to **±5°/decade**, and its level at mean log frequency clipped to the corridor. This is an explicit placeholder, not an owner-approved slope bound; the line is not constrained to stay inside the corridor at each frequency. The `dead` total applies raw 1 dB ripple, 0.5 dB DI roughness and 1 dB flatness free limits before tolerance normalization, plus strict constancy. The `all_dead` variant also replaces v9's squared DI-slope error with squared distance from the placeholder 0–3 dB/decade range, using stored DI and the same slope routine. Loading is already dead-banded by v9; flat CV and constancy remain active, so zero ties do not represent a configuration in which every term is dead-banded.

Sensitivity shifts both corridors together by ±5° and independently changes V onset to 700, 1,000 or 1,250 Hz. Only the canonical coverage term needs recomputation for those cases: width stays 20°, so canonical ripple and other v9 inputs are unchanged. For the first three rows each case was checked against a full v9 score at numerical tolerance 1e-10. Tables collapse repeated attempts with the same run and design hash; across-run observations remain separate because response/fidelity can differ.

The historical 0.17 J screen/verify spread was measured under a different score and is not a validated noise floor for this new score. Small rank gaps should not be treated as established acoustic differences.
