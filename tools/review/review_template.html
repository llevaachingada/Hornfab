The problem

What is being optimised

A horn is a parametric surface. A design vector x (5 to 11 searched numbers, depending on the family) is meshed, solved with a boundary-element method at 12 log-spaced frequencies, and turned into polar cuts (73 angles from −90° to +90°, horizontal and vertical, at 2 m) plus a 512-point Fibonacci balloon. From those, six per-frequency metrics are computed, and CMA-ES minimises one scalar J. Lower is better.

J(x) = wcov·cov + wrip·rip + wdi·di + wslope·dislope + wflat·flat + wload·load + penalty
cov = (1/100)·meanf∈B[(θh(f) − θh*)² + (θv(f) − θv*)²]
rip = meanf∈B[σh(f) + σv(f)]
di = (1/10)·meanf∈B[(DI(f) − DI*)²]    dislope = (s − s*)², s = slope of DI vs log10f
flat = stdf∈B[SPLaxis(f)]    load = meanf∈B[max(0, zmin − Re Zthroat(f))]
B
The target band, e.g. 700–5500 Hz. Solved frequencies outside it are shown in plots but never scored.
θh, θv
−6 dB beamwidth (total included angle) of each cut; the edge is the exact half-pressure level 20·log100.5 = −6.0206 dB.
σh, σv
Ripple: sample standard deviation (ddof = 1) of the on-axis-normalised dB inside the target coverage, ignoring samples more than 40 dB down.
DI
10·log10(paxis² / mean p²) over the full-sphere balloon, averaged in linear power. DI* comes from the target angles: Ω = 4·arcsin(sin(θh*/2)·sin(θv*/2)), DI* = 10·log10(4π/Ω).
weights
Default cov 1, rip 1, di 0.3, dislope 0.5, flat 0.5, load 0 (throat-load term off in every run so far).
penalty
10³ for a design that fails to mesh or solve. Finite on purpose: an infinite value breaks the CMA-ES covariance update.

Reading of the results

What the data says

The pipeline works end to end

4,018 solves, 125 failures (3.1%), every one classified. Searches improved on the seed in every run, by 8% to 78%. The solver agrees with itself and with an independent solver to well under the size of the improvements.

Three of four early searches hit a wall, not an optimum

OSSE pinned n at its lower bound in all six best designs; ICW pinned hold_end (6/6) and nearly kappa0 (4/6); the throat adapter pinned three of five parameters. Their best J values are lower bounds on what those families can do, and the search space should be widened before more GPU time is spent on them.

The first FREEFORM setup searched almost nothing

With 3 free parameters the sensitivity of every one is near zero (|ρ| < 0.07): a flat plateau, 8% gain in 722 solves. The 11-parameter sculpt version gained 22% in 60 solves with no failures and nothing pinned.

Given 400 solves, the sculpt search found its own wall

J fell from 30.9 to 16.8 (−46%) with 1 failure in 400. The best designs push the wall radius at 145 mm depth to its lower bound in both planes and the vertical mouth radius to its upper bound: the optimiser wants a slower flare through the middle and a larger mouth than the configuration allows. The configuration keeps each wall anchor in its own non-overlapping radius range so the wall can never pinch; relaxing that needs a monotonicity constraint instead of box bounds.

The surrogate model is not ready

A neural surrogate trained on 1,501 OSSE records passed 5 of 6 accuracy gates but had too few held-out designs (285 of the 500 required) and one non-monotonic sweep. It is not used. It needs about 1,900 more OSSE solves, roughly 10 GPU hours.

For the reviewer

Questions worth a mathematician’s time

  1. Is a weighted sum the right scalarisation? The six terms have different units and scales (cov is deg²/100, rip is dB, dislope is (dB/decade)²). The weights were set by hand. Would a normalised, Chebyshev, or Pareto (NSGA-II is already in the code) formulation give more defensible optima?
  2. Is the ripple metric well posed? A sample standard deviation over angles inside the target coverage, which itself depends on the target. Does that make J discontinuous as the beamwidth crosses the coverage edge?
  3. Beamwidth is a threshold crossing. θ(f) jumps when a side lobe crosses −6 dB. Is there a smoother surrogate quantity (energy-weighted width, a moment of the pattern) that CMA-ES would handle better?
  4. How should bounds be chosen? Several runs are bound-limited. Is there a principled way to set bounds from the mesher’s validity region rather than by hand, or to let the search reparametrise? For the sculpt family in particular: the wall radii must increase with depth. A reparametrisation in positive increments (ri+1 = ri + eui) would enforce that without the disjoint boxes that now pin the search.
  5. Is 12 log-spaced frequencies enough? Narrow resonances between samples are invisible to the objective. What sampling density would bound the error in each term?
  6. Surrogate acceptance. The gates require Spearman > 0.9, top-20 overlap ≥ 12, beamwidth MAE < 2°, DI MAE < 0.3 dB, and zero monotonicity flips on 1-D sweeps. Are these the right tests for deciding that a surrogate can replace the solver inside the loop?
